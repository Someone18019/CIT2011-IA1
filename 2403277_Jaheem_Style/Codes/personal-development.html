Personal review of The Richest Man in Babylon

Seven Cures Reflection

"The King realizes that giving people gold directly does not solve poverty, because the gold
simply passes through their hands and ends up back with the few who already understand
how to keep it. He asks Arkad to teach people how to acquire wealth instead. This connects
to my own life because I’ve realized that being placed in a good position or given an opportunity,
whether at school or at work, is not enough on its own. If I don’t take the time to understand
that opportunity and learn how to hold onto it, I’ll lose it the same way the people of Babylon
lost their gold. The lesson learned is that I need to actively learn how to make the most of my
opportunities, not just receive them."

The Laws of Gold

"My thoughts on the five laws are about how I place my money, or my sources of funds.
Something I’ve already made for myself into a potential opportunity that I don’t have full
understanding of. In a sense, I placed myself into the school of learning computers,
computer science and networking, without fully understanding what exactly I’m getting
myself into. I’m investing in something I don’t fully understand, and as such I am losing
my investments. For me to get any better, I need to take my time to learn and understand,
and have patience, discipline, and dedication towards this craft, in order for it to potentially
benefit me later on."
